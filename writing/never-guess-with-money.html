Never guess with money: why a payment system must be able to say "unknown"

By Oguh Chidiebube Kenneth · 21 October 2026

You ask a bank to pay ₦50,000. The connection drops. No answer comes back. Did the payment happen?

You do not know. And that is the hard part of money software: there are two easy answers, and both are wrong.

  • Say "failed" and try again. If the first payment did go through, you have now paid twice.
  • Say "paid". If it did not go through, the supplier is unpaid and your records are wrong.

The honest third answer

A payment system needs a third state: unknown. It means: "I asked, I did not get an answer, and I am going to find out." It is not a failure of the system. It is the system refusing to pretend.

A payment is not a yes/no value. It moves through states, and one of them is "I do not know yet". In Zentrix, a payment that is in that state is marked as an unknown outcome, and the dashboard says so: "outcome unknown, being checked".

How "unknown" gets resolved

  • One key per payment. Every payment carries a unique key. If you ask again with the same key, you get the same payment back, not a second one. Asking twice is harmless.
  • Check, do not retry blindly. The system asks the bank what happened, and settles the record when it knows.
  • Switch providers only when sure. If a provider fails and you are certain nothing was sent, trying a second provider is safe. If the outcome is unknown, switching is exactly how a payment gets made twice. Zentrix switches only in the first case.

Why this matters for anyone building data systems

This is a data-modelling lesson as much as a payments one. If a field can only be true or false, the system has to lie whenever the truth is "not known yet". Model the third state on purpose, and a whole class of money bugs disappears.

It matters most wherever bank connections are unreliable, which is why we are building for Nigeria first.
